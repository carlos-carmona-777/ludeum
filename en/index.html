The museum halls

The games are grouped by era and place, as in a museum. They are rebuilt from ancient sources, archaeological finds and oral tradition, and when the sources disagree you choose the variant: Ur with Irving Finkel's rules, 9 × 9 Tablut or 11 × 11 Hnefatafl, Oware or Kalah, and more.

Antiquity

c. 2600 BC – 6th c.

Mesopotamia · c. 2600 BC

Royal Game of Ur

The Twenty Squares

A race with pyramid dice over 20 squares: rosettes grant another roll and captures happen in the middle row.

Their rivalsLudingira, the apprentice scribe · Ea-nasir, the merchant · Puabi, the Queen of Ur

2 variants

Greece · 7th c. BC

Pente Grammai

The Five Lines

The oldest Greek board game we know of: bring your pieces to the sacred line.

Their rivalsDafnis, the shepherd · Nicias, the hoplite · Aristóteles, the philosopher

2 variants

Rome · 1st c. BC

Latrunculi

The Game of Soldiers

Place your soldiers, then trap enemies between two of yours to capture them.

Their rivalsDécimo, the recruit · Lucio, the centurion · Máximo, the legate

3 variants

Rome · 1st c. BC – 6th c.

XII Scripta

Twelve Lines · Alea

A 15-piece dice race, ancestor of backgammon: bear them all off before your rival.

Their rivalsFélix, the freedman · Fortunata, the barmaid · Claudio, the emperor

2 variants

Rome · Imperial era

Three Men’s Morris

Roman three in a row

Place and move three pieces to form a line. Quick and deceptively deep.

Their rivalsQuinto, the boy from the Forum · Rufo, the innkeeper · Cornelio, the senator

2 variants

Middle Ages

4th–15th c.

Scandinavia · 4th–11th c.

Hnefatafl

The King’s Board

An asymmetric Viking game: the defenders escort the king to a corner; the attackers try to surround him.

Their rivalsLeif, the fisherman · Sigrid, the skald · Harald, the jarl

2 variants

Rome · Middle Ages

Nine Men’s Morris

The Mill · alquerque of nine

Place and move nine pieces: every three in a row makes a mill and lets you remove a rival piece.

Their rivalsRodrigo, the squire · Brother Tomás · Alfonso X, the Wise

2 variants

Peoples of the World

Traditions from around the world

Africa · traditional

Oware / Mancala

The Sowing Game

Sow seeds from pit to pit and capture your rival’s when you leave 2 or 3. Whoever gathers 25 of the 48 wins.

Their rivalsKofi, the boy · Nana Kwame, the chief · Yaa Asantewaa, the queen mother

2 variants

Mesoamerica · until 1521

Patolli

The Game of Beans

An Aztec race on a cross-shaped board with five beans as dice: bet jade beads, avoid the triangles and capture in the center.

Their rivalsTochtli, the macehualli · Itzel, the pochteca · Moctezuma, the huey tlatoani

2 variants

Hawaii · before 1778

Kōnane

Lava and Coral

A Hawaiian jumping game: the board starts full and every move captures. Whoever can no longer jump loses.

Their rivalsKai, the fisherman · Leilani, the weaver · Kaʻahumanu, the regent

2 variants

Greece · Byzantium – today

Tavli

Greek backgammon

The backgammon of Greek cafés: three games in a row on the same board, with hitting, trapping and blocking, up to 5 points.

Their rivalsArchimedes, the mathematician · Aspasia, the Milesian · Achilles, the hero

4 variants

Card Room

16th–19th c.

England · 17th c.

Cribbage

Fifteens, Pairs and Pegs

An English card game with a pegboard: score fifteens, pairs and runs, and reach the finish before your rival.

Their rivalsNed, the potboy · Moll, the barmaid · Sir John, the poet

2 variants

Mexico · 19th c.

Conquián

The Grandfather of Rummy

The Mexican card game that gave birth to rummy: meld the center card in sets and runs until your hand is gone.

Their rivalsToño, the muleteer · Doña Chole, the cook from the fonda · Don Juan, the rancher

2 variants

Play your way

  • Single playerAgainst characters from the era, from Easy to Hard.

  • OnlineWith Game Center: a random rival, or invite your friends with a link or a code. Live or turn-based, at your own pace.

What we know and what we don't

Every game comes with its “i” cards: History, with photos of real boards and pieces from museums; How to Play, with an animated explanation; and Rules, which separate what is documented from what is still debated. A guided tutorial teaches you to play the first time.

Challenges, tournaments and achievements

  • Challenges

    The Weekly Challenge and the Monthly Challenge, with stamps for your yearly collection.

  • Weekly tournaments

    The Weekly League and the Game of the Week, with Game Center leaderboards. They start on Monday, November 2, 2026.

  • Achievements

    35 Game Center achievements for exploring the halls, playing online and beating the toughest rival in every game.
